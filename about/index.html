About SpendMath

SpendMath is a free, private budget calculator. Add your monthly bills, see where your money goes, and get practical ideas to keep more of it. No account, no bank login, and your numbers never leave your device.

Who runs SpendMath

SpendMath is designed and run by Muhammad Ishaq, a UI/UX designer based in Karachi, Pakistan. I design apps and websites for a living, and I built SpendMath because most budgeting tools ask for too much: an account, a subscription, or access to your bank. I wanted something simpler: type in your bills, see the full picture in a minute, and get clear ideas you can act on today.

I design and maintain the calculator, keep the country figures checked against the official sources listed on the methodology page, and update the guides when figures or rules change.

Why it’s free and independent

SpendMath is independent. It isn’t owned by, or affiliated with, any bank, lender, insurer or financial company. No company pays to be mentioned, and the savings ideas never push a particular product or provider.

The site is free to use. To cover its running costs, it may show ads from Google AdSense. Ads never change the calculator’s results or what the guides recommend.

How the calculator works

You enter your monthly bills, from rent and power to loans and subscriptions, and the calculator compares each one with typical amounts for households like yours in your country. Where a bill looks high, it suggests ways to spend less, and every idea shows the exact sum behind it under “How did we get this?”. Only the ideas you add to your plan count towards your results. Interest you would avoid on debt is shown separately and isn’t counted as money saved each month.

Your privacy

Your budget is worked out in your own browser and is never sent to us. There is no account and nothing to sign up for. Ads keep the tool free. Read the full Privacy Policy.

Editorial standards

SpendMath is for education and planning, not personal financial advice. Typical amounts and savings estimates are approximate, and every source is listed on the methodology page. If something looks wrong or out of date, tell me and I’ll check it and correct it. Guides show the date they were last updated.
